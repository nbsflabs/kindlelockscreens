Questions and answers

Compatibility

Will it work on my Kindle?

It works on Kindle e-readers that have a setting called Display Cover. Amazon has added it to most Kindle e-readers sold in recent years, including Paperwhite, Oasis, Scribe, Colorsoft and the standard Kindle. To check, open Settings on your Kindle and look for Display Cover under Device Options, or under Screen and Brightness on newer models.

It does not work on Fire tablets or in the Kindle app on a phone.

What are lock screen ads, and why do they matter?

Some Kindles are sold at a lower price “with ads”, which Amazon also calls Special Offers. Those Kindles show adverts on the lock screen, and the Display Cover setting is not available on them. Amazon lets you remove the ads for a one-off fee from the device settings in your Amazon account. Once they are gone, Display Cover appears.

Will it fit my screen?

Yes. Pick your e-reader at the top of the maker and the lock screen is made at its exact screen size, from 600 × 800 for older basic Kindles up to 1860 × 2480 for the Kindle Scribe. The preview shows exactly what will appear.

Does it work on Kobo or with KOReader?

Yes. Choose your Kobo, or “KOReader or a jailbroken Kindle”, in the maker and you get a picture file sized for your screen instead of a Kindle book. You can also download every design at once as a ZIP, ready for a screensaver folder that shows a different one each time. The setup guides explain where to put the files.

Does it work in colour?

Yes, on a Kindle Colorsoft. Picking one of the colour designs turns Grayscale off for you. For a colour photo, turn off Grayscale in the maker before you download. Other Kindles show everything in shades of grey.

Using it

How do I get it onto my Kindle?

  1. Make your lock screen and tap Download.
  2. From a phone or tablet, tap Send to Kindle app and pick the Kindle app from the list. On a computer, download the file (it ends in .epub) and send it with Amazon’s Send to Kindle, on the web or by email.
  3. When it appears in your library, open it once.
  4. Turn on Display Cover in Settings.

The next time your Kindle goes to sleep, it shows your lock screen.

What happens when I read another book?

Display Cover always shows the cover of the book you opened most recently. While you are reading something else, the lock screen shows that book’s cover. To bring your lock screen back, open the lock screen file again for a moment before you put the Kindle to sleep.

Why is it a “book”?

Kindles do not have a setting for choosing your own lock screen picture. They can show the cover of a book, so we make a tiny one-page book whose cover is your picture.

Which photos can I use?

JPG, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC photos. Photos with strong contrast and a clear subject look best on e-ink. Turn on Smooth gradients for photos with big skies or soft shadows: it dithers the picture to the 16 shades of grey an e-ink screen can show, so they don’t break into bands.

Can I add my contact details in case I lose my Kindle?

Yes. After choosing a photo, type a line in Add text, such as “If found, please email me@example.com”. It is drawn onto the picture on your device and is never sent to us.

Privacy

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Your photo is opened and edited inside your browser, and the Kindle file is made there too. Nothing you make is sent to us or stored by us. See the privacy policy.

Free and open source

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Every design and every download is free, with no account, no ads, no tracking and no subscription.

Is the code public?

Yes. Kindle Lock Screens is open source: the whole site is on GitHub. You can read how it works, report a problem or suggest an improvement there.

Can I download my lock screen again?

Nothing is stored, so keep the files you download. You can always make the lock screen again in a minute.

What if it does not work on my Kindle?

Get in touch and we will help you get it working. Email our email address.

Can I use the designs for anything else?

Yes. The designs are open source along with the rest of the site, under the MIT licence: use, change and share them however you like, as long as the licence notice goes with them.
